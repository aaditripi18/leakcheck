01The problem

A high score can be a lookup, not a detector

Security benchmarks make high scores easy: on a random split of CIC-IDS2017, untuned baseline models reach 97.1–99.9% macro-F1. Many benchmarks put identical or near-identical rows on both sides of the train/test split, mix future data into training, or contain a single column that gives the label away.

A model can then score by recognising rows it has already seen. That says little about how it handles traffic or files it has not seen, which is the thing a deployment needs.

What leakcheck measures
How much of a benchmark's score is earned on rows the model has already seen, and how much remains once they are removed.
02What it checks

Six checks on every split

L1 · EXACT COPIES Test rows identical to a training row, after canonical hashing.
L2 · NEAR COPIES Test rows within a tiny distance of a training row (tabular), or Jaccard >= 0.8 (text, MinHash + exact check), compared with how close training rows sit to each other.
L3 · LABEL CONFLICTS Identical features with different labels, and the accuracy ceiling they impose.
L4 · TIME LEAKAGE Training rows newer than test rows, and the timestamp as a one-feature classifier.
L5 · SHORTCUTS A single feature that predicts the label on its own, and identifier-like columns.
L6 · GROUP OVERLAP The same host, family or session on both sides of the split.

leakcheck clean removes the leakage, then re-reads its own output and re-checks every row. If anything remains, it fails.

05Pre-registered

9 hypotheses, decided by rules fixed before the runs

H1
At least half of the benchmarks leak ≥ 5% of their test set into training. 5 of 8 datasets.
SUPPORTED
H2
Removing the copies costs ≥ 5 macro-F1 points on the two most-copied benchmarks. KDD99 loses 15.0–17.5 points; UCI Phishing loses at most 1.7.
NOT SUPPORTED
H3
A time-based split hurts more than removing copies. EMBER: the time split costs 1.9–2.6 points vs 1.3–2.4 for removing near-copies; a margin of 0.3–0.9.
SUPPORTED
H4
Weaker models lean harder on leaked rows. LR gap > HistGB gap on 6 of 6 eligible datasets; the paired 95% interval is above zero on 4 of 6 (KDD Cup 99, NSL-KDD, UNSW-NB15, EMBER 2018).
SUPPORTED
H5
Some benchmark is solved by a single feature. PhiUSIIL: URLSimilarityIndex alone reaches 99.6% balanced accuracy.
SUPPORTED
H6
Tuning and all the training data do not make the copies stop mattering. Tuned gradient boosting on all 4,898,431 KDD Cup 99 training rows still loses 22.2 points; NSL-KDD 8.6. Every seed's interval is above 5.
SUPPORTED
H7
Weaker models lean harder on copies, with intervals and a tuned model. Logistic regression's gap exceeds tuned boosting's on 4 of 4 large benchmarks, with the paired interval above zero in every seed.
SUPPORTED
H8
Whether a gap clears one point does not depend on the near-copy threshold. The same in 32 of 40 cells; every change is a gap near one point. The large gaps hold at every threshold.
NOT SUPPORTED
H9
On network traffic, a time split costs more than removing copies. Raw UNSW-NB15: the time split costs 0.2–0.5 points, removing near-copies 4.3–6.5.
NOT SUPPORTED

Each decision rule was committed to PLAN.md before the experiment it judges was run.

06What we learned

Four findings

FINDING 01

Copying is not the same as inflation.

UCI Phishing copies about as much as KDD Cup 99 (64.4% vs 68.4% of test rows) but loses only 1.5 macro-F1 points when the copies go, against 17.5 for KDD Cup 99. With 30 features of 2–3 values each, a UCI Phishing copy is just a common pattern a model gets right anyway; KDD Cup 99's copies are memorised exceptions, and 73% of its gap comes from near-copies rather than exact ones.

Logistic regression: KDD99 −17.5 vs UCI −1.5
FINDING 02

The control comes back clean.

EMBER 2018's official split is by time. leakcheck finds 0.009% exact copies, 0.0% of training files newer than the test period, and test rows further from training than training rows are from each other (×0.79). The audit does not flag everything.

EMBER 2018: 0.009% copies · no future data
FINDING 03

Too easy to rank models.

Every baseline scores at least 99.99% macro-F1 on PhiUSIIL. Removing the four features that each reach 95% on their own still leaves at least 99.95%. A test that easy cannot tell models apart.

Best single feature: URLSimilarityIndex 99.6%
FINDING 04

Random splits can hide a collapse.

On CIC-IDS2017 the baselines score 97.1–99.9% macro-F1 on a random split, and 46.2–56.5% when trained on earlier capture days and tested on the last ones, whose attack types (PortScan, DDoS) never appear in training.

Gradient boosting: random split 99.9% → later days 46.2%

09Scope

Limitations

  • A copy is not always a bug: real traffic repeats. Both the original and the cleaned score are reported.
  • The cleaned test set is harder by construction; the gap measures how much of a score was earned on copies.
  • Near-copy thresholds (0.01 RMS, Jaccard 0.8) are fixed choices; a curve and a training-set baseline are reported alongside.
  • Baselines are untuned standard models, and training sets are capped at 300,000 rows.
  • EMBER is scanned on leakcheck's own numeric view, not the official 2,381-dimension feature vector.
  • CIC-IDS2017's time split is confounded with unseen attack types, so H3 was decided on EMBER.
